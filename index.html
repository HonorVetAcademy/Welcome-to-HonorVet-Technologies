Chapter 4 of 14

How we got our certifications

Being veteran-owned is a status. These are what make it official, and add a few more on top.

You’ll be able to: name HonorVet’s four certifications, when we earned each, and what each actually required of us.

When a hospital or a client hands us a requisition, they’re trusting us with something serious. These certifications back that trust up, and they’re the reason clients can verify, not just take our word for it.

SDVOSB

Veteran-owned status

Certified by the SBA and NaVOBA.

At least 51% ownership and control by one or more service-disabled veterans, certified through the SBA’s Veteran Small Business Certification (VetCert) program, self-certification alone is no longer accepted.

It unlocks eligibility for federal set-aside and sole-source contracts, part of the government’s 3% contracting goal for SDVOSBs.

JCC

Joint Commission Certified

The same bar hospitals hold themselves to.

Acquired 2021–2022

Formally the Joint Commission’s Health Care Staffing Services Certification, built for firms placing temporary clinical staff into direct patient-care settings.

Standards cover Leadership, Human Resources Management, Performance Measurement & Improvement, and Information Management. A passing review earns the Gold Seal of Approval.

ISO 9001

Quality management

Processes that stay consistent.

Achieved 2025

Not staffing-specific, the global standard for quality management, used across every industry.

Requires documented, repeatable processes, regular internal audits, and continuous improvement, checked by an independent third-party auditor.

ISO 27001

Information security

How we protect your data.

Achieved 2025

The global standard for information security management, how risk to data is identified and controlled.

Requires formal risk assessment, documented security controls, and regular audits. Covers everything from a candidate’s Social Security number to a client’s contract terms.

Chapter 7 of 14

What is our business, exactly?

Two words you’ll hear constantly, and the clients those words apply to.

You’ll be able to: tell recruitment and staffing apart, and tell government and commercial clients apart.

These two words get used interchangeably, but they mean different things day to day, and recruiters are usually the ones who have to explain the difference to a client.

Recruitment

The process of finding, attracting, screening, interviewing, and selecting candidates for a job.

Finds and hires talent

Staffing

Determining the need for a role, recruiting and hiring the candidate, onboarding them, and then managing, training, and retaining that workforce over time.

Manages the workforce, recruitment included

And who are we doing this for? Every requisition you work traces back to one of two kinds of clients, and they behave very differently. Government work moves on regulation and procurement rules. Commercial work moves on speed.

Government

  • Federal — agencies like the Department of Defense, Homeland Security, NASA, and the IRS. Contracts follow Federal Acquisition Regulations (FAR), with large contract values and extensive compliance and security requirements.
  • State — agencies like the California and Texas Departments of Transportation, plus state universities and public authorities. Procurement rules vary by state, with a focus on regional services and infrastructure.
  • Local — city and county governments, school districts, and public utilities — think the City of New York or Los Angeles County. Smaller budgets than federal agencies, with local procurement processes.

Commercial

  • Private-sector businesses of every size: startups, small and mid-sized businesses, and large enterprises.
  • Faster purchasing decisions driven by profit objectives, with contracts typically more flexible than government work.

Together, Federal, State, and Local — plus Education — form what the industry calls SLED. At the top of the commercial side sits the Fortune 500, the 500 largest U.S. companies, ranked annually by revenue by Fortune magazine. Names like Walmart, Amazon, Apple, UnitedHealth Group, Berkshire Hathaway, CVS Health, ExxonMobil, Alphabet (Google), Microsoft, and JPMorgan Chase represent that scale.

Why this matters to you

A federal RFP and a commercial inquiry need completely different turnaround expectations. Knowing which bucket a client falls into tells you how to pace the work.

Contingent Staff Augmentation

Companies use contingent labor instead of hiring directly, going through a staffing firm for contract professionals that bridge skill gaps, support project delivery, and add operational flexibility while minimizing permanent hiring commitments.

Permanent Staffing

The process of bringing together a job seeker and a prospective employer for the purpose of a traditional employment relationship, for a fee, placing full‑time employees onto the client’s own payroll long‑term.

Payroll Services

Administering employee compensation accurately and on time, in full compliance with labor and tax law.

  • Leave & attendance integration
  • Statutory filings

Project Services

End‑to‑end professional support to plan, execute, manage, and deliver a business or technology project within a defined scope, timeline, and budget, through skilled resources, structured methodologies, and governance frameworks.

Chapter 13 of 14

The language you’ll hear

A handful of acronyms that will show up before your first week is over, so let’s get ahead of them.

You’ll be able to: correctly use RFI, RFP, RFQ, RFR, Single/Multi Award, and CTH in context.

RFI

Request for Information

A client is gathering information on vendors’ services, capabilities, and rates, usually not an immediate hiring order.

RFP

Request for Proposal

A formal ask for a full proposal: solution, pricing, experience, approach. Often part of a competitive procurement.

RFQ

Request for Quotation

The client mainly wants pricing for specified services or positions; requirements are usually more defined than an RFI.

RFR

Request for Resume(s)

The client or prime vendor wants candidate resumes matching a role, this is the closest to an actual staffing opportunity.

SA

Single Award

One vendor wins the entire contract.

MA

Multi Award

Several vendors are awarded, then compete against each other for individual task orders.

CTH

Contract-to-Hire

A candidate starts on a temporary contract, with the possibility of converting to a permanent employee after a defined evaluation period.
